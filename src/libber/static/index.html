One-time setup

Reading your playlists needs a free Spotify API app. Takes about two minutes, and nothing here is sent anywhere except to Spotify.

  1. Open the Spotify Developer Dashboard and click Create app.
  2. Name it anything. For Redirect URI paste exactly: …
  3. Tick Web API, save, then open Settings to reveal the client ID and secret.

Spotify requires a signed-in account to list playlist contents — public playlists included, not just your own. Albums and single tracks work without it.

Reusing a Spotify app from another project? Add … to its Redirect URIs first (Dashboard → your app → Settings → Edit). Adding one is safe — the URIs your other project uses keep working.

Settings

Higher threshold = fewer wrong versions slip through, more tracks parked for review. 70 is a reasonable middle.

Opus is near-transparent by about 128 kbps in stereo, so High mostly buys file size — worth it only for dense or difficult material on good headphones. Neither setting re-encodes; they just pick which stream YouTube hands over. High needs cookies.

YouTube answers anonymous requests with “sign in to confirm you’re not a bot”. Cookies from a browser you’re signed into get past that. They are read locally and go nowhere but YouTube — libber never stores or logs them, only the browser name and profile path above.

Use a spare Google account for this. Cookies from a signed-in browser attach every download to that account, and Google restricts accounts it decides are automating downloads. A blocked IP clears in a few hours; a suspended Google account takes your mail and everything else with it. Signed out, or a browser profile with no Google login, is safest — you may still get past the check, and you risk nothing but the IP.

Downloading a long playlist flat out is what gets a connection blocked. A second or two between tracks, with fewer in parallel, keeps you under the radar on big runs.
